# 19. Evenings

By day goods move. From 17:00 folk move. This doc is where the two lanes meet (P2): the places the Hearth composes, the round residents make of them each evening, what they buy on the way, and what good evenings turn into over the seasons.

**Status**: accepted by the owner on 2026-11-10. R2 builds places, the round, evening trade, good evenings, evening streets and gaps; R3 builds supplies, masters and the Town Book (`30-roadmap.md`). It stands on the zones-first Ledger (`13-ledger.md`). Every number in this doc is a starting value *(tune)* unless it says "as built".

**Why** (measured 2026-11-10 with the autoplayer, 70 days, Village rank granted on day 19 in every run because the Charter's own Hearth goals would otherwise block it):

| Seed | Hearth lane played | Hearth lane off (no decor, wishes, patterns, nature, festivals or working together) |
|---|---|---|
| willow | 157 residents, 416 coins, mood 93 | 166 residents, 444 coins, mood 80 |
| hazel | 126 residents, 452 coins, mood 88 | 106 residents, 470 coins, mood 82 |

The town grew about the same without the Hearth. The reasons were in the rules: decor was free and charm was never spent; from Village rank mood no longer touched money; the one hard gate (charm 25 at the door for Craftsfolk) could be met with flowers by the door; requests had left the interface. Meanwhile the sim already walked every resident to a bench, well, tree or tavern chosen by charm, species, traits and friends (`systems/residents/outings.ts`), and nothing economic read it. This doc makes that walk the Hearth's economy.

## The loop

Goods make places come alive. Places make good evenings. Good evenings make friends, couples, bonds and, in time, masters. Masters and newcomers make the workforce and what it can make. That makes the goods.

**Volume is the Ledger's, variety is the Hearth's.** Land, streets, specialized buildings and trade decide how much there is. People and places decide what kinds there are and whether anyone enjoys them. Tiers ask for both (`13-ledger.md`, Population tiers).

## The day in two acts [R2]

- Work hours stay 08:00 to 17:00 (builders and porters until 19:00). The evening is 17:00 to 21:00, and on festival nights to 22:00.
- **The evening lingers** *(tune; a valley setting, on by default)*: from 17:00 to 21:00 the clock runs at half the chosen speed, as the night already runs three times as fast. An evening then lasts 160 seconds at 1x instead of 80, through golden hour and dusk, the best light the game has. A turn advances half its ticks, rounded down on odd turns and up on even ones.
- *As built (R2B)*: `ticksThisTurn` (`speed.ts`) runs `lingerPercent` of the effective speed's ticks from `eveningHour` to `homeHour` while `settings.eveningLingers !== false`, rounded down on odd turns and up on even ones by the turn number, so it hashes on turns and ticks alone and a save mid-evening resumes the same; `ClockView.lingering` tells the top bar. At 1x the evening takes 1,600 turns. Tests that count turns as ticks wrap themselves in `withoutNightHurry`, which leaves out the linger as it does the night's hurry.

## Places [R2]

A place is a spot where folk spend time. Places are derived, never built as such: the sim finds them hourly with the patterns pass and after a Hearth command nearby.

**Where places come from**

- Every standing pattern site (`12-hearth.md`, the Pattern Book).
- A sitting spot: any seat piece that belongs to no pattern site, together with the other such seats within 3 tiles of it, up to four seat pieces *(tune)*. So every bench is somewhere to go, and more benches in a row make more places, not one endless one.
- A porch: a home with a porch (the dressing ladder, or one fitted by hand) has 2 seats for its own household, with the street's charm as its draw. Sitting out front is a modest evening that grows with the town by itself.
- Hosts: the tavern, the Market Square and each stall, the well, the Bath House, the skating ice of a frozen river, a bonfire or brazier, and the festival site on its night. The brazier is a Village decor piece from R2 (room for 8 standing, warm), lit for nothing until R3 gives fires a supply, so winter evenings are possible from the start.

**What a place has**

- **Seats** from its pieces (`DecorDef.seats`): bench 2, curved bench 3, ring bench 4, a low wall segment 1, cafe table 4, arbor seat 2, lookout bench 2, swing and see-saw 2 children each, a festival's trestle table 6 *(all tune)*. Hosts: tavern 12, Bath House 8. **Standing room** where folk gather on their feet: a well 4, a stall 4, the ice 12, a fire 8. A walk (below) holds one stroller for every 2 tiles. Beyond its seats a place has standing room for half as many again (at least 2). A stop lasts about an hour, so over an evening a seat serves three or four residents: a town wants about one seat for every four grown-ups *(tune)*, and a level 2 shop with room at its door puts a table out by itself, so seats also grow with the town.
- **Kinds**, one or more: *sit*, *eat* (an outlet serves it), *view* (water within 3 tiles, or a lookout), *play* (a play piece), *meet* (a well, square, green or market), *walk* (a linear pattern: Lantern Walk, Promenade by the Water, Hedgerow Lane, Orchard Path, Bridge Crossing).
- **Shelter**: the tavern, the Bath House and the Market Hall are sheltered and warm. A place with a lit fire is warm.
- **What it serves**: the outlets with Treats in stock within 3 tiles (a shop door, a stall, the tavern).
- **Draw**, 0 to 100 *(tune)*: its charm (the average over its tiles, counted up to 70), plus 8 for a pattern site, 2 for each kind of piece in it (at most 8), 5 for water within 3 tiles, 5 after 18:00 when its lanterns are lit, 4 when it serves Treats. Charm is most of it on purpose: the flat parts alone never make a fine evening. In winter an open-air place with no fire and no ice draws 20 less.

A place is named for what stands there and the nearest family, as Chronicle places are ("the bench by the Underbank cottage"), and either seat may rename it.

*As built (R2A)*: a sitting spot covers the tiles around its seat pieces, so a flower bed or a lantern beside a bench counts among its pieces and lights it; a fire within 3 tiles of a sitting spot joins and warms it, and stands alone otherwise; a host that is a member of a pattern site (a well in the Well Gathering Place) is part of that site; on the festival's night its site is a place of its own with its pieces' seats and room for 40 standing; the skating ice is a place at each skating spot while the river is frozen; a porch has no standing room. Names: with no family near, a place is named for a street beside it ("the bench on Mill Lane"), else the water, else "at the edge of town"; a second bench by the same cottage is "the other bench".

*Determinism (R2A)*: the place index is rebuilt whenever a structural signature of its inputs changes (decor, buildings with their trade and whether each sells Treats or has tables out, homes and households, pattern sites, the ice, the festival), checked at most once a tick and turn; the draw's moving parts (charm, lanterns, winter, and the tree and quiet taste bits) are recomputed each tick. A rebuild keeps each place whose draft and inputs did not change since that state's last build, and the porch, host and outlet drafts while the buildings, households and sites did not change; a state with no last build (a seat joining) finishes every place, to the same result. So every client reads the same places at every point of the sim, a seat joining from a snapshot included. `sellsTreats` and `cafeTables` must never read the place index. "Tonight" for a resident is `tonightStops` (the evening's stops planned today, after any afternoon's).

## The round [R2]

- Between 17:00 and 17:30, at their own minute, every young and adult resident plans an evening of one to three stops (`chooseLeisure`, grown into a plan). Builders and porters plan at 19:00.
- **Choosing**: for each place within their leisure radius (12 tiles; Curious 18; Homebody 6) the score is its draw, plus their taste for it (species and traits as built, from minus 10 to plus 15), plus 8 when a friend is going, minus 0.35 a tile of distance (Manhattan, as `chooseLeisure` measures today, so no route is searched to choose), minus 20 for every seat's worth of crowd beyond its room, plus a little whim. A place that scores under 20 for them is not worth the walk: they stay in. The best is the first stop, but one resident in two (by id and day) takes their second best first, so a town does not all arrive at one bench at 17:00. A second and third follow while time allows, each the best place of a kind not yet visited within 8 tiles of the last.
- A stop lasts about an hour, as outings do. They set off for home at 21:00, as built.
- **Shopping comes first**: a resident whose pantry is low shops at the start of the evening, as built, and the shop or stall is the round's first stop; they go on to a place if time allows.
- **An evening at a friend's home** is a stop with a draw of 30 and no bonus for meeting the friend, so visiting is pleasant and still no substitute for somewhere to go: it cannot by itself reach the 50 a Craftsfolk household wants.
- **Seats** are taken on arrival, then the standing room (10 off the evening for standing). A place with neither left sends the latecomer on to their next choice, or home.
- **Routes**: the walk grid gains an evening cost, in whole numbers and never under a tile's ordinary cost (so the search stays as fast as it is): pleasant tiles (charm 40+, or an evening street) cost what they always do, plain tiles a quarter more and tiles with noise 3+ half more *(tune)*. Folk take the pleasant way when it is not much longer. The evening grid is fixed at 17:00 each day, so lanterns lighting and carts passing never throw away the evening's cached routes.
- **Weather** as built: in rain one resident in two skips open-air places; in a storm everyone stays in.
- Children play at play places and near home as built. Elders and residents without work also make a round in the afternoon, and their best outing of the day counts.
- The festival is the season's great round and keeps its own rules (`12-hearth.md`).

*As built (R2A)*: the planner gives each stop the hour it sets off (`RoundStop.at`): a place full at the first hour may have room at a later one, a little less tempting for each hour of waiting (`waitPerHour`), so a seat serves three or four residents an evening and folk wait at home for their hour. They judge a place as it will be when they get there: planned at 17:00, a place with lanterns counts its lit part for any hour from 18:00, so a lantern-lit town fills from 18:00 and its 17:00 hour stays quiet. Hours follow the resident's own clock (a Night Owl's evening starts later, an Early Bird's ends sooner). The crowd penalty counts the residents planning a place for that hour. Everyone plans at their own minute even when a storm, the bell or the festival keeps them in, so the tally counts every grown-up; on the round, rain and the ice are the plan's to weigh (the rain-shy plan sheltered places only, the ice is a place like any other). Elders and residents without work plan an afternoon round at 14:00 (`afternoonHour`), whose stops come first in the day's list (`Resident.roundEvening` marks where the evening begins) and are not an evening's visitors; their walks count for the day's footfall. A sheltered host (the tavern, the Bath House) is spent inside. Children go to a swing or see-saw within 6 tiles of home one time in two when they play.

*Routes as built (R2A)*: the evening grid's classes (pleasant, plain, noisy, evening street) are fixed at 17:00 and saved (`EveningsState.routes`, dropped at 04:00), so a seat joining mid-evening routes as its partner does; A* runs at four times the cost scale with the heuristic scaled to match. Barrows read the live evening flag from 17:00 to 21:00.

**Footfall**: every step a resident takes adds one to the tile's count for the day (`map.footfallDay`); steps on the way to a stop or between stops of a round count for the evening instead (`map.footfallEve`), and the walk home from work or to bed does not, so a street is busy of an evening only when it leads somewhere; both halve at 04:00, so each reads as about two days of walking. The `wear` layer keeps its own job (desire paths).

## Evening trade [R2]

Treats leave the 18:00 household pass and are bought on the round.

- **In a Hamlet** treats are free at the shelf, as food is (wages and prices begin at Village rank, `13-ledger.md`, Budget), so the round is learned before money moves. Everything below about paying holds from Village rank.
- **At a stop that serves Treats**, a resident whose household is still owed a unit that day (the basket's Treats line, accrued as built) buys the flavor they like best from the shelf, pays at the till and has it there, in hand and on the table for anyone to see. A comfortable household (savings above its mark, as built) buys one more.
- **Passing trade**: walking within a tile of an outlet with Treats, a resident still owed one stops and buys one time in five *(tune)*.
- **From the doorstep**: a household none of whose members bought on the round sends to the nearest shop in reach at 21:30 for a third of what it is owed (in hundredths, as the basket accrues), eaten at home without much ceremony. So a town with no places still sells some Treats and nothing dead-ends.
- Food, Fuel and Wares are bought by day as built (`13-ledger.md`, Households).
- Every sale counts toward the outlet's custom (business levels) and the place's line in the Hearth's book.

**Shops follow the walkers.** From R2 a Shops lot's desirability gains up to 30 for evening footfall at its frontage (full when a tenth of the town's grown-ups pass *(tune)*) and 10 for a place within 3 tiles. The Shops demand bar counts each place that drew 8+ visitors with nothing to sell them, beside the households no outlet reaches. A shop of level 2 or more whose door is within 2 tiles of two or more seats puts tables out and becomes a cafe: its tables join the place and it serves there. The Ledger zones shops along the Hearth's walks, or the Hearth builds places by the shops. P2's own example is now literal: merchants will not settle on a street nobody walks.

*As built (R2B, 2026-11-16)*, where the rules above needed a call. Every number is in `content.places`.

- **When Treats move.** The `baskets` system runs every 4 ticks (a walker stands at least 4 ticks on a tile, so the pass sees every tile one stands on). At 17:00 each household's Treats line accrues its day in hundredths (it no longer accrues or buys at 18:00, which still records Treats' demand for prices); from 17:00 to 21:00 the trade pass sells at stops and to passing trade; at 21:30 the doorstep errand runs and the line settles: what fell due and was not bought is let go, and the line's satisfaction moves halfway to the share bought, as the household pass did.
- **At a stop** (the last stop arrived today, not turned away; `placeServes` for a place, the shop itself for a `shop:` stop) a resident whose household is owed a whole unit buys one, at most once an evening; a comfortable household's buys one more, not owed. The flavor is the household's best liked (R1A's likes; liking per till price from Village rank, skipping what the purse cannot reach), ties to the lower outlet id and good index. The treat is in hand (`Resident.carry`, the `carry` pose) until they walk on, go in or 21:00; it is eaten, never put in the pantry.
- **Passing trade** rolls a hash of resident, outlet and day, never an RNG stream: one roll per resident, outlet and evening, however many passes see them by the door, so the odds do not depend on how long they stand there. It counts against a stop still ahead, whose `treat` flag it sets.
- **The doorstep** keeps its third in `Household.doorstep` (hundredths): a household that bought nothing on the round adds a third of each unit that fell due and sends for a whole one when it has 100, to the nearest outlet in reach (an outlet's food reach, or 14 tiles) with Treats on the shelf, then the next in reach. Doorstep sales count in `treatsDoorstep`, toward the outlet's custom, not toward a place's takings.
- **Which sells Treats** (`sellsTreats`): the tavern, the stall, the Market Square, and a shop whose level's shelf lists Treats (level 2 on). Storehouses, granaries and the Trading Post are outlets but never sell to the round.
- **The round and the carrier share one stock, in two buffers.** The carrier comes at 10:00 and takes surplus only from makers' output buffers and the storehouses, never from a stall's, shop's or the tavern's shelf; the round takes only from those shelves (store less what porters or the carrier reserved). Porters restock the shelves by day; at 21:30 the doorstep errand goes on to the next outlet in reach when a shelf is empty.
- **Shops follow the walkers.** Evening footfall at a frontage is read as half the `footfallEve` layer (it halves at 04:00, so it holds about two days); a place within 3 tiles counts unless it is a porch. A place counts toward the Shops bar (`unservedPlaces`, the reason beside P2's) when its last closed evening drew 8+ with nothing served and no stall, square, shop or tavern stands within `serveRadius` of it (a shop there levels up on footfall instead). Growth grows a shop by it without a rule of its own: each such place stands in for `SHOP_HOUSEHOLDS` homes no outlet reaches (`unservedDoors`), so the shop demand, the lot choice by reach and then desirability, and the hands gate all apply as to any shop. Every ten evening steps past a shop's door (half the layer at its front tile) are a customer for its level. A shop of level 2 with two grown-up seats within 2 tiles of its door is a cafe from the 23:30 levels pass (`Building.cafe`, `BuildingView.cafe`); `cafeTables` gives the cafe table's seats.
- **Garden lots** are never priced, never a free lot in any of the bars' reasons and never opened by a shopkeeper.

## Good evenings [R2]

- **An evening's score**, 0 to 100, for each resident: the draw of their best stop as they found it, plus 8 for meeting a friend there, 8 for a treat, 4 for a second kind of stop, less 10 for standing. Staying in scores 20, and 20 is every evening's floor, so going out is never worse than staying home. An evening lost to weather does not count.
- **The evenings need** takes the place of `beauty` in mood (weight 15) and of `social` for Craftsfolk. It starts at 50 for a newcomer, and at 21:00 it moves a third of the way to the evening's score. Charm round the door still feeds home desirability, arrivals and the dressing ladder as built, and no longer opens a tier by itself.
- **Tiers**: a household's grown-ups must average evenings of 50 for Craftsfolk, 65 for Merchants and 80 for Patrons, three days running *(tune)*. A flower by the door cannot do that; a place with a seat in reach can.
- **Friends** are made as built (2 points an hour outdoors at the same spot), so places make friendships and friendships make couples.
- **Bond**: a resident with four of a season's six evenings at 60 or more gains a heart *(tune)*, once a season, up to 5. Wishes still give hearts up to 3, festivals and long residence as built.
- *As built (R2B)*: `eveningScore` takes the stops arrived today (not turned away): the best of draw, plus `friendScore` for a friend there (not at a friend's home), less `standingPenalty` for standing there; plus `treatScore` for a treat tonight (one bought passing counts once they got somewhere); plus `secondKindScore` for two kinds of stop (a shop stop is no kind); between `stayInScore` and 100. `closeEvening` at 21:00 scores every grown-up, elders included (an elder who stays in scores the floor like anyone; children keep their need); a storm loses the evening (nothing moves); the need moves `Math.round((score - need) / 3)`; the scores join `tonight.scoreSum` only once R2A has rolled tonight's tally. In mood, evenings weigh `needWeight` (15) for every tier and stage, beauty 0 and the tavern's `social` 0 (`weightsOf`). Tiers: Craftsfolk ask their grown-ups (every member but children: a lone elder had passed the gate unasked) to average evenings of 50 (`NeedsBasket.evenings`) for `tierDays` days running; staying is forgiven `eveningsSlack` (10; R2X: 15); the tavern and charm 25 have left the basket. Until R2A's round lands nobody goes out, every evening scores 20 and nobody reaches Craftsfolk.
- **The evening's tally** (numbers, not a book): the share of grown-ups who went out, the average evening, each place's visitors, seats and takings, and the friendships made. It feeds the Hearth's strip, the place card and the evening edition (`17-presentation.md`).

## Evening streets [R2]

`road.setEvening { tiles, on }` marks road tiles for strolling. From 17:00 to 21:00 carts and porters' barrows treat them as closed and go round (one with no other way waits at the edge, and its shed or store says so), no cart noise falls on them, and walkers prefer them. Carters keep the porters' hours, to 19:00. The cost is real: a longer haul, and a stall or workshop on the stretch gets no delivery after 17:00. The gain is a calmer round. Both show in the two inks.

*As built (R2B)*: closed from `eveningHour` to `homeHour` whatever the linger setting. A cart's route keeps off a closed evening street but for the tile it stands on; the route cache keeps the open and the closed routes apart and drops both when a street is marked. A cart whose route ahead enters the stretch at 17:00 goes round or stops at the edge; a cart with a job under way and no way round keeps its load and reservations, waits, and goes on at 21:00 (`BuildingStatus.eveningWait` on its shed); no new haul starts that cannot set out. Porters plan with `pathFor(..., 'barrow')` while the street is closed and, with no barrow way, wait with their task (`eveningWait` on their store). No porter or cart job runs to or from a door on a closed evening street, and that building reads `eveningStreet` ("On an evening street: no deliveries from 17:00 to 21:00"). Cart noise never falls on an evening street, at any hour. The cart shed's carter keeps the porters' hours, to 19:00.

## Gaps: requests that write themselves [R2, R3]

P13's requests return without a form. A gap opens by rule, shows in both lenses (the left feed and a small paper tag on the ground), says in words what would close it, closes itself, and credits in the evening edition the seat that built the piece that closed it (once a place a season, so nothing is gained by pulling a stall down and putting it back). Gaps open from Village rank; a Hamlet has enough to learn.

| Gap | Opens when | Closes when | Reads like |
|---|---|---|---|
| A place with nothing to sell [R2] | 8+ visitors on two evenings running and no Treats within 3 tiles | an outlet serves there for two evenings | "The Promenade drew 14 folk and had nothing to sell them." |
| A shop nobody passes [R2] | Treats on its shelf and under 4 customers on three evenings running | a place within 3 tiles draws 8+ | "The Underbank shop sold two ciders in three evenings: nobody walks this way." |
| Nowhere to go [R2] | 6+ households stayed in three evenings running for want of a place in reach | half of them go out | "Mill Lane stays in of an evening." |
| A place short of supplies [R3] | its pieces rested two nights running | supplied for two nights | "The Lantern Walk wants 6 candles a night; the town makes 2." |

The `request.*` commands stay for free-text asks, without the ask form. Closed gaps count where fulfilled requests did (Together goals, Odette's arc).

*As built (R2C)*: the pass runs at 21:10, on fair evenings only (rain and storm hold every streak), and reads each rule over the evening just closed. "Customers" for a shop nobody passes are the visitors at the places within 3 tiles (per-outlet evening sales are not saved), and "draws 8+" is 8 visitors. Credit goes to the seat whose command built the piece; a business grown on a lot goes to the seats in the Ledger lens. A kind the town cannot meet opens nothing (its open gaps still close), a gap whose subject is gone is dropped without credit, and the audit flags an open gap nobody can close (`gap-dead-end`). The Village's evening goal counts fair evenings running: a fair evening below half breaks it, rain holds it. Details in `docs/build/cycle-r2c-gaps-book.md`, As built.

## Supplies: places come alive [R3]

Some pieces draw on the town's goods to be at their best, from Village rank on (a Hamlet's lanterns light for nothing). Homes are served first, then businesses, then places: a place draws only on what the town holds above its reserves and winter marks, and the carrier never buys for places unless the Ledger turns that on for the family (`carrier.setRule`). So a fire never takes a home's firewood, and the Balances book shows what places draw each night. A piece without its supply **rests**: it stays where it is, costs nothing and loses only what the table says. Nothing breaks and nothing is taken away (P5).

| Piece | Draws on | How much | Resting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lantern, hanging lantern, lamp post | candles (the Old Age's "oil lanterns" burn the chandler's candles) | 1 a night for every 4 | unlit: its day charm only, no night draw, no deterrence |
| Bonfire, brazier | firewood | 1 on an evening someone comes, autumn to the thaw | cold: the place is not warm |
| Cafe table, trestle table | the outlet beside it | its Treats | bare: seats only |
| Bunting, flower arch, maypole, festival stage | cloth | 2 when put up, once a season | faded: half its charm |
| Fountain, birdbath | a well within 8 tiles | nothing | dry: half its charm |

*Ruled (2026-11-17)*: there is no bandstand piece and no musician's evening, so R3 has no Bandstand row; `bandstand-green` stays an unlock, and the bandstand waits for I4 with the theater and the inn. The trestle table is the festival table. A cafe or festival table moves no stock and keeps no ledger of its own: it is served while an outlet within 3 tiles has Treats on its shelf (read live, as R2 reads it), and folk buy from the outlet as R2B built; otherwise it rests bare, seats only. **The tavern's Treats** (the owner's ruling): the tavern sells what it pours from its own input (cider and the like, delivered as today), and that pouring stock counts as its Treats on the round, for the tables beside it too: no second shelf, no new chain. **Fires in winter**: places draw only above the reserve and the winter mark, but for places the mark falls through winter (the mark times the winter days left over the winter's length, derived, not saved), so a town that entered winter with its reserve met keeps its fires, and a short town's are cold; the carrier's places switch is the other way out.

- **The lamplighter**: one job at the Town Hall for every 40 lanterns (the ceiling of supplied lanterns over 40, at least one from Village rank once a lantern stands, none in a Hamlet). With nobody in the job, the lanterns the town has candles for still count from 18:00 and come on together with no walk, and the Town Hall says the job is open: the job is the show and the work, never a way to darken a town. At 17:30 he takes the night's candles from the nearest storehouse and walks the lanterns, the places marked first before the rest, nearest first. Which lanterns get a candle is settled at 17:30 from stock and priority, and for the rules (charm, draw, raiders) those count as lit from 18:00 sharp, so a raid at dusk never finds a town half lit. The walk is what the players see: each lamp comes on as he reaches it. Lanterns go out by themselves at 07:00.
- **Windows**: every occupied home keeps the glow it has today (a share of its windows, by the hour). A home with a candle lights all of them until bedtime, with the lamplit rooms behind (`17-presentation.md`); one candle lasts a home five nights *(tune)*, bought within its Wares (a Wares flavor like any other, inside its budget and counting for variety; no line of its own). So a well supplied street is visibly brighter, a town short of candles dims home by home, and both players can see why. No home ever goes dark.
- **Which places first**: `place.setPriority` (keep first, as it comes, let it rest). This is the Hearth's own small economy: what to keep alive tonight. The fourth gap credits the seat whose priority (keep first, on that place) or carrier places switch (for that family) was set last while it was open; with neither, the seats in the Ledger lens.
- The chandler's candles come from honey, and hives want flowers (`12-hearth.md`, Nature): a loop through both lanes.

## Masters [R3]

- **Becoming one**: a young or adult resident with 4 or more hearts and 20 working days in one trade (apprentice days count double), in a business of level 2 or more or at a specialized building *(tune)*. One new master a trade a season, apprentices included. A sign over the door, a Chronicle line and a letter.
- **What a master brings**:
  - their business may reach level 3 (`13-ledger.md`, Levels);
  - a **master's good**, made from the same raw good in the same building and sold like any flavor. Nine in R3 *(content, tune)*: the baker's pies, the dairy's butter, the orchard's jam, the cider maker's cordial, the beekeeper's mead, the weaver's coats, the carpenter's toys, the chandler's soap, and the Fishery's smoked fish;
  - apprentices: a young resident who serves a season under a master becomes one after 10 working days of their own, so a trade spreads (no four hearts asked: the master's bond is how a trade spreads).
- Masters of the other trades and buildings bring something that is not a good *(content)*: the quarryman's carved stone and the forester's ornamental trees are pieces for the Hearth; the miller makes room for one more baker; the grain farmer, the gardener, the shepherd and the shopkeeper let their business reach level 3 and train apprentices.
- **Losing one**: when a master's household leaves the valley, or the master retires, the business keeps its level for six days and then slides, and the master's good stops when the trade has no master left. So the Ledger has reason to care about Hazel by name, and to ask the Hearth to look after her.
- Story characters are masters by their arcs (`11-residents.md`).

*As built (R3B, 2026-11-22)*:

- **Working days** (`Resident.craft`) are counted at 23:45 from the crews, for the trades and specialized buildings that have masters only (`content.masters`): one a day on the crew of a built, unpaused building that is not resting out of season, two for an apprentice serving under a master; a day out of season counts nothing and loses nothing, and days are never taken away.
- **Becoming one**: a young or adult worker, not a story character, on the crew of a business of level 2 or more (or at a specialized building) with 4 hearts and 20 days in its trade, or a finished apprentice of a master with 10 working days of their own since (`Apprenticeship.ownDays`); one a trade a season (`MastersState.lastMade` by absolute season, so `MASTERY.perTradeSeason` stays 1), the most days first, then the lowest id. A sign (`Building.masterId`, kept in step with the crew each night), a Chronicle line, a letter and a life event; the first quarryman's and forester's masters unlock the carved stone and the ornamental tree (`masterDecor.ts`, on existing kits until R3F).
- **The master's good** is made by the master's own hands: while a master is inside, their work goes to the master's recipe on its own counter (`BuildingRuntime.masterProgress`) and the crew's to the trade's, so it is never a multiplier; when the master's recipe cannot run (no raw good, a full buffer) they work the trade's. A gatherer's master (the orchard, the hives, the Fishery) takes the raw good from the building's own output, and that use counts as custom for the level. The nine goods sit in `SHELF_GOODS`: a household sees, counts and buys only what is on a shelf in reach, and asks for (the demand tally) only a master's good the town has now, made or in stock (`masterGoodsInTown`), so an unmade good keeps its base price and a lost master's good stops being asked for once the last of it is sold.
- **Level 3** needs a master of the trade on the crew to climb (`canReachLevel3`). A level 3 business with no master on its crew keeps its level `MASTERY.graceDays` (6) days from the night it first finds none (`BusinessCounters.masterGone`), then slides one level as any business slides; a migrated level 3 (`levelKept`) holds without a master until one works there. The miller's master makes room for one more baker in the windmill's reach (`masterRoom`).
- **Keeping one**: the job rebalance leaves a master at a building of their own trade, as it leaves an apprentice to see a season out (a master off the crew brings neither level 3 nor the good); the hiring feed (`tierShortages`) names a master's good short only while the town has it.
- **Apprentices** start under a master without a school: a young worker on a master's crew whose trade takes apprentices, or a young resident who starts the school way at a workshop with a master. A finished apprentice of a master who still works in the trade stays put rather than moving to a skilled opening.
- **Losing one** is told at 06:00: a master who retires that morning, or whose household the morning pass marks as leaving (they walk out at 09:00), gets a Chronicle line and a life event (`masterLost`). A retired master keeps the title.
- **Story characters** are masters by their arcs, once the arc is done (`StoryArc.mastery`, read when `StoryState.step` passes the last step): Barnaby Ashcombe the quarry's, Pippa Quill the carpenter's, Odette Fenwick the shopkeeper's, Mother Thornbury the gardener's; Juniper Reed waits for I4. The town's line says the arc made them one (`master-story`). They count for Master of the House, not against a trade's season. (Masters from arrival, tried first, forked the autoplayer's first days: no rank changed over eight more seeds, but oak ran into Village debt by day 70.)
- **The pie contest** is offered in spring, summer and autumn once a master baker makes pies (`masterMakes`), and asks for 6 pies on the menu; any menu may carry pies (Food and Treats).
- *As measured* (the autoplayer, R3B's branch before P3A): the first master by the rule came on day 40 on willow (Village day 19), 23 on birch (31), 38 on oak (39), and 45 on hazel (Village day 19), three days past its first Village year. Hearts are not what holds a town back (hazel had 33 grown-ups at 4 hearts or more); 20 working days in one trade are, as hands move between jobs and farms rest in winter. The first masters are mostly at specialized buildings (the Forester's Lodge, the Fishery), which ask no level.

## The Town Book: specialties and customs [R3]

A shared book in both lenses, kept like the Pattern Book: found pages, and hints for three not yet found.

- **A specialty** is a flavor the town is known for at one place: sold there to 10+ customers on four evenings of a season *(tune)*, it gets a name and a page ("Orchard Walk Cider": cider at an Orchard Path). About 20 pairs of pattern and good in R3 *(content)*. A specialty counts as one more flavor for households whose round passes the place, sells to neighbors at their full price, can go on a festival menu as a dish of its own, and adds 5 to the place's draw.
- **A custom** is a habit the town falls into: the same place drawing 20+ folk on the same day of the season three seasons running (a day lost to weather holds the run), or a supply kept every night of a season ("the Lantern Round"). On its day the place draws 15 more, and a custom can be a festival's twist.
- **Credit** goes to both seats when the place and the trade's zone or specialized building were laid by different seats ("Made Together"). A page is worth 2 renown, as a pattern is.
- **Callings are earned here**: each page leans to industry, gardens or pilgrimage, and three pages of one leaning earn the town that calling (`15-progression.md`); a town may earn several.

*As settled in R3.0 (2026-11-17)*: the nine masters' goods are pies (Treats), butter (Food), jam, cordial and mead (Treats), coats, toys and soap (Wares) and smoked fish (Food), none sold by a neighbor; with the base flavors that is Food 8, Wares 6 and Treats 6 against the Merchants' 5, 3, 3 and the Patrons' 7, 5, 5, before specialties and trade. Until a master makes one, a master's good is no flavor anybody wants, counts or misses. The Town Book holds 21 specialties (pairs of pattern and Treat, since the round sells Treats) and 13 customs. A Hamlet's free lanterns do not count for the Lamplighter's Round. Which pieces rest tonight, a place's warmth and its specialty and custom parts are derived each tick, never saved. The contracts are in `20-architecture.md`, R3 contracts; the rulings in `docs/build/r3-wave-notes.md`.

## Data and budgets

- **A place has a stable id**: its pattern and anchor tile for a pattern site, the lowest id among its seat pieces for a sitting spot, the building's id for a host.
- **Saved**: `map.footfallDay` and `map.footfallEve` (16 bits a tile), the `evening` flag on road tiles (the `flags` layer, R2.0), each resident's evenings need and last score, working days by trade (`craft`, kept across winters and changes of work) and the master flag, lantern lit bits, Town Book pages, open gaps, and for each place by id its name, its priority and the counts that rules read over time (visitors and takings for the last six evenings, the running counts behind gaps, specialties and customs). From R3 also the door-pair counts behind Follow the thread. **Derived** and rebuilt on load and each hour: a place's seats, kinds, draw and what it serves. A seat joining from a snapshot therefore sees exactly what its partner sees: as built (R2.0), a place is a pure function of saved state when it is read, and whether an outlet has Treats on its shelf is read live, never cached.
- **Budgets** (`22-quality.md`): the place pass under 1 ms an hour at Village scale and 3 ms at City scale; planning the round under 0.4 ms a tick for 2,000 residents, spread over the half hour from 17:00; footfall adds nothing measurable to a step. All of it is deterministic sim code and hashes with the rest.
