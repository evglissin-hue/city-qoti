## Try during the evening

1. Start from an I1 valley file or a new standard valley. Grow to 30 residents, then open the Charter board (H) and work through its five goals together. Claim it, and enjoy the proclamation.
2. Ledger, from Village rank:
   - Paint zones (homes, shops, crafts, farms) along a lane and watch lots appear and the village grow its own homes and shops overnight.
   - Build a Trading Post and set a standing order or two (Trade book).
   - Open the Market (M): watch a price move and read why.
   - Try the wage and income tax dials (Policies, P), and take a small loan.
3. New chains:
   - A clay pit, worked for two days, invents the Brick Kiln.
   - Sheep, a spinner and a weaver for cloth.
   - An orchard and a cider press for treats.
   - A tavern and a herbalist near the homes.
   - Watch for the first Craftsfolk townhouse rising under scaffolding.
4. Hearth:
   - Read the story letters (O) from Pippa, Odette and Juniper, and help them along.
   - Follow a couple to their wedding.
   - Find one of the six new Pattern Book pages.
5. Together:
   - Sketch a plan in blueprint ink for your partner to approve.
   - Send them a request for goods or charm, and watch it fill itself.
6. Look and feel: walking residents, carts with their ponies, sheep and cows, cobbled streets once Cobbles is invented, lanterns at night, the minimap.

## Known going in

- Craftsfolk come slowly. In the autoplayer's 90-day runs some seeds raise a few dozen and some none, mostly because their timber or cloth chain stalled. Tell us how it felt to chase them by hand.
- Village money is tighter than a Hamlet's: from Village rank, upkeep is paid to the village's own households and income tax replaces the flat tax, so the treasury lives on sales, taxes and trade. Loans should come down again once the chains run.
- Seasons, weather, festivals, threats and the Watch arrive in I3.

- Which story letter landed, and which fell flat?
